# Corpus-wide cross-source highlighting

Date: 2026-10-11. The owner reported that Sahidic, Vulgate and Syriac highlighted alone in Matthew 3:1, required the correction throughout the corpus, and explicitly included Bezae Latin correspondence with Greek.

The first release hid existing comparison alignments by default while showing the small set of contextual decisions and Greek form matches. That default did not provide the expected cross-source reading interaction. This decision supersedes that default in the word-stream decision; no transcription, source selection, English allocation or semantic certification rule changes.

Existing comparison links now participate by default across all stored verses. Dashed highlights and a visible explanation identify these associations as still under review. Readers can turn them off. This restores access to the stored alignments without relabeling positional evidence as scholarly certification. Empty, omitted, lost and unpopulated cells are never highlighted. A known source-specific word remains isolated.

Bezae Latin is matched to unambiguous Vulgate Latin occurrences using case, accents, ligatures, i/j and u/v comparison. Repeated forms use the same conservative occurrence matcher as Greek. Such occurrences share their corresponding comparison group even when their stored row or source order differs. A displaced form match takes priority over its old positional pairing; an unmatched competing word does not get added to the same witness slot. This same precedence now applies to displaced Greek form matches. No source spelling or ordering is changed, and no English-text matching is used.

Documented contextual groups take precedence. A single-anchor group can provisionally include an existing counterpart from an unrepresented source, but never a competing occurrence from a source already represented by that decision. Multi-anchor semantic groups are not propagated through other semantic groups. Exact Latin form matches remain form evidence, and links inherited from a comparison row remain provisional.

Matthew 3:1 adds fingerprint-guarded occurrence decisions for the reported failure, including Latin days/connector displacement, Bezae Latin John and preaching, Sahidic Baptist/preaching/wilderness, and the Syriac verbal unit. The original source text and English allocations remain intact. These are project-authored contextual decisions, not independent scholarly certification.

Validation includes hover interaction outside the contextual examples, bidirectional displaced Bezae Latin/Greek correspondence, the reported Matthew 3:1 examples, source-specific and subset exclusions, and corpus preservation and precedence checks for all 3,779 verses. The audit distinguishes documented/form coverage from provisional-only coverage. Full semantic review of existing comparison links remains separate editorial work.
