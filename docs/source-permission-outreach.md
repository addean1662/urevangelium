# Source-permission outreach

These messages are prepared for the project owner to send. No request has yet been transmitted by Urevangelium.

## Logos — SCNTE transcription and permission

**To:** permissions@logos.com
**Subject:** Permission inquiry: George Horner Sahidic Coptic New Testament in English (SCNTE)

Hello,

I maintain Urevangelium, a free, noncommercial public digital-humanities project that displays the textual history of the Four Gospels across several manuscript and language traditions.

We are evaluating the Logos resource *Sahidic Coptic New Testament in English (SCNTE)* by George W. Horner (resource/product 7369). Horner's printed work is public domain, but we do not assume that purchasing the Logos edition authorizes extraction or redistribution of Logos's digital transcription.

Could you please clarify:

1. Was the SCNTE English text produced by human transcription, OCR, or another process?
2. What quality-control or source-verification process was used?
3. Does the resource also preserve or link the exact Horner Coptic text underlying each English passage?
4. Would Logos permit Urevangelium to extract, align, and publicly redistribute Horner's verbatim English for the four Gospels, with full attribution, in a free noncommercial website and public data repository?
5. If permission is possible, may the resulting alignment data include the verbatim English, verse references, translation-unit boundaries, and provenance hashes?
6. What license terms, attribution, fee, or permission identifier would apply?

Urevangelium will not redistribute Logos markup, software, metadata, or other proprietary content unless specifically authorized. We are willing to limit use to the public-domain Horner wording if Logos can license its transcription for this purpose.

Thank you.

## CrossWire — CopSahHorner provenance

**To:** modules@crosswire.org
**Subject:** Provenance inquiry for CopSahHorner module

Hello,

I maintain Urevangelium, a free public digital-humanities project comparing the Four Gospel traditions. We are investigating the CrossWire `CopSahHorner` module as a possible comparison source for George W. Horner's Sahidic Coptic edition.

The module information identifies the source as “Slavic Bible for Windows.” Could you please provide any surviving provenance records for this module, including:

1. the contributor or producer;
2. whether the Coptic was human-keyed, OCR-derived, or imported from another digital source;
3. the exact Horner volume/edition used;
4. any correction or quality-control history;
5. the original source files or repository, if preserved;
6. the basis for its Public Domain designation; and
7. whether the module may be redistributed as structured source data in a free public repository.

We will not treat the module as authoritative without a traceable provenance chain and facsimile auditability. Even partial historical information would be useful.

Thank you.

## Coptic SCRIPTORIUM — Sahidica public-use clarification

**To:** contact@copticscriptorium.org
**Subject:** Sahidica NT 4.1.0 permission clarification for free electronic Gospel edition

Hello,

I maintain Urevangelium, a free, noncommercial public digital-humanities project displaying the textual history of the Four Gospels across multiple traditions.

The Sahidic column pins Sahidica NT 4.1.0 as distributed through Coptic SCRIPTORIUM. It displays 48,275 normalized Gospel word-groups with source-file, occurrence, version, and hash provenance. We clearly identify Sahidica as an edited electronic corpus rather than a single manuscript, and we disclose that SCRIPTORIUM's segmentation and linguistic annotation layers are automatic.

Your Sahidica licensing page states that the edition is available free of charge for use in free electronic editions of the New Testament when the full title and copyright information are included and credited. Urevangelium is free to readers and appears to fit that permission.

Could you please confirm whether that permission covers:

1. public display of the pinned Sahidica 4.1.0 Gospel word-groups;
2. storage of the pinned source files in a public source repository;
3. publication of derived occurrence-level alignment data and source hashes; and
4. retention of version 4.1.0 for reproducibility rather than silently updating to a later release?

Please also advise the exact attribution and copyright notice you would like displayed, and whether repository redistribution requires any additional permission.

Thank you.
